6.2

Limitations

Methodology and interpretationNot supplied to models

From the methodology, §5.4 What this research can establish

The study can document observable problem-formulation, cross-domain interpretation, evidentiary restraint, and judgment in an open-ended written task. It cannot prove internal cognition, human-like understanding, or reliable discovery in unrestricted data. Larger records and external validation would be necessary to investigate those further claims.

From the methodology, §5.3 Principal threats to validity

The chief danger is measuring conformity rather than reasoning: a rigid answer key can reward familiar themes and suppress independent judgment. Further threats include author anchoring on the membership theory, scorer overlap across dimensions, verbosity, prompt sensitivity, case familiarity, and generalization from a single fictional polity. The case joins several data sources that have different units and sample populations; falsely treating them as linked would produce unwarranted certainty. The single-assessor pilot cannot establish inter-rater reliability. Fluency is not depth, and a strong essay does not prove stable performance across domains. Construct validity requires ongoing scrutiny of what the rubric actually measures (Jacobs & Wallach, 2021).

The case name is a further risk. Nordland is also the name of a real county in Norway, a country with its own documented history of assimilation policy toward Sámi and Kven people, and a model may import that history. The model-facing record states that Nordland is fictional and confines the analysis to the record; importing outside facts about a real place as evidence is scored under Dimension IV.

From the validity memorandum

Research boundary: The audit can support claims about written problem-formulation and judgment under a documented protocol. It cannot establish internal reasoning processes, consciousness, a uniquely correct explanation of Nordland, or stable general-purpose conceptual intelligence from one small pilot.
