Section 02

Methodology

How the study is designed: the questions it asks, the protocol for collecting unassisted responses, what conceptual reasoning means here, and the validity questions the project must answer.

2.1

Research questions

Primary research question. How can a strong welfare state sustain significant inequality despite its commitment to universal social protection?

Embedded evaluation question. Can an AI model confronted with a complex but bounded record identify what most needs to be understood, discover consequential relationships, consider competing interpretations, and reach a defensible judgment without the assessor specifying the intellectual route?

Nordland is a fictional welfare state. Its measurable redistributive achievements, unequal initial benefit-denial rates, high public support, and weaker minority-background belonging can coexist. Their coexistence is not a logical contradiction. It creates an intellectual problem about what counts as universality, access, fairness, and institutional success. The welfare-state question remains the substantive center of the research; AI evaluation is the secondary, inseparable measurement question.

2.2

Conceptual reasoning evaluation

An accurate list of observations is not automatically a consequential analysis. The research distinguishes five intellectual activities:

  1. Problem formulation: identify the governing question rather than simply repeating the most striking statistic.
  2. Conceptual distinction: ask whether entitlement, administrative access, receipt, material redistribution, and civic belonging are the same thing.
  3. Relational discovery: find links, counterexamples, or independent processes that change how a theory should be organized.
  4. Evidentiary calibration: distinguish description, association, experimentally supported effects, proposed mechanisms, and unresolved causal links.
  5. Substantive judgment: explain which interpretation or next investigation deserves priority and why.

These are assessment lenses, not directions given to the model. A strong response might propose a different, well-supported framework that an evaluator did not anticipate. Originality is valuable only when it improves explanation, produces a discriminating question, or supports a better justified judgment.

2.3

Study design and protocol

  1. Supply the fixed, neutral case record. The model receives the Nordland case and broad essay assignment, not evaluator interpretations, theories, examples, or rubric.
  2. Collect an unassisted response. Preserve the complete prompt, model/run identifier, date, relevant settings, tool availability, and unedited output. The essay is limited to 1,500 words, with charts, tables, diagrams, graphics, and non-linear representations permitted; all visible text counts toward the limit.
  3. Assess the completed artifact. Score five distinct dimensions from 0–4 using passage-specific rationales. The five-dimensional profile is primary; the total out of 20 is a secondary summary.
  4. Protect interpretive independence. Do not reward agreement with an evaluator's favored answer, mandatory mention of named facts, rhetorical fluency, or speculative novelty. An unexpected but sound interpretation can earn full marks.
  5. Calibrate and validate. Compare divergent high-quality arguments and weak but polished ones. Additional assessors should score independently and, where feasible, blind to model identity. Preserve initial scoring disagreements and examine agreement and run-to-run variation in future studies.
Separation of materials

The model-facing record is a standalone factual document. Interpretive notes, the rubric and constructed examples reside separately in the evaluator materials and must not be supplied to a model during the initial test.

2.4

Why the evidence is difficult

The record mixes distinct units of analysis: nationwide poverty rates, benefit claims, office-level characteristics, a probability survey, a voluntary website poll, a randomized notices experiment, a résumé audit, and contested historical interpretation. Those populations are not person-level linked. A causal effect of translated notices in an enrolled language group is not the same claim as a causal effect of discretionary interviews, an explanation of nationwide denial disparities, or a historical explanation of civic exclusion. The model is free to discover which distinctions matter; the evaluator must check whether conclusions respect the evidence.

2.5

Validity questions

The project must substantiate two claims separately: the Nordland record is an internally coherent, genuinely open-ended problem, and the scoring process measures demonstrated conceptual reasoning rather than conformity to an evaluator's expectations.

Case and evidence validity

  • Numerical integrity: Do the 16 offices' claim counts, group shares, and weighted denial rates reconcile with nationwide figures after rounding? A preliminary audit of the case record finds approximately 150,000 claims, a 19.92% minority-background share, and weighted initial denial rates of approximately 18.29% and 8.94%. Keep a reproducible verification script alongside the source.
  • Units and populations: Separate claims from people, office data from individual cases, resident attitudes from benefit applications, minority home language from minority background, and name-cue résumés from actual claimant identities.
  • Causal scope: Distinguish the randomized translated-notice comparison from observational office differences and proposed multi-institutional pathways.
  • Genuine alternatives: Can administrative design, case composition, language access, historical continuity or discontinuity, employment conditions, and belonging support distinct plausible accounts? Which records would distinguish them?
  • Counterexamples: Does a response use Office 6 and Office 12 thoughtfully, rather than treating them as automatic proof or disproof of a theory?
  • Sampling and missing evidence: Do the voluntary website poll, lack of appeals data, unobserved eligibility, repeated claims, and missing person-level linkages change the interpretation?

Construct and scoring validity

  • Self-directed inquiry: Is the model deciding what to investigate, or merely following a checklist?
  • Interpretive plurality: Can very different well-supported accounts score equally high? Can an unanticipated insight receive full credit?
  • Dimension separation: Are scores for problem formulation, conceptual reasoning, relationship discovery, evidentiary discipline, and substantive judgment justified independently?
  • Top-score integrity: Does a rating of 4 represent a genuinely consequential analytical step beyond a strong 3, rather than polished prose or novelty alone?
  • Equivalent media: Are diagrams, prioritizations, concise essays, and text-heavy outputs evaluated by the same substantive standards?
  • Rater and run variability: Do ratings agree across independent assessors, and do model patterns persist across repeated unassisted runs?
  • Exposure risk: Could a public model retrieve evaluator notes? Maintain new held-out cases for subsequent validity research.

Immediate research sequence

  1. Preserve a reproducible case-table numerical audit and an evidence/denominator ledger.
  2. Confirm that the case page contains only the neutral record and broad prompt.
  3. Collect actual unedited model completions and metadata; distinguish them explicitly from constructed calibration examples.
  4. Independently assign five scores with cited passages and reasons, then compare strong and weak arguments.
  5. Audit accessibility, links, score arithmetic, and consistency before public presentation.
  6. Expand to additional raters, repeated runs, and held-out cases only when the initial protocol is demonstrably coherent.

Reporting boundary: The pilot can demonstrate a method for scoring written problem formulation and judgment. It cannot establish internal model cognition, a uniquely correct explanation of Nordland, or stable general-purpose reasoning ability from one small case.

The cited works motivate methodological questions; the Nordland observations themselves are stipulated fictional data and should not be mistaken for findings from these works.
